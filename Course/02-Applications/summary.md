## AI is software

- Anthropic and OpenAI each offer a chat app, a work app and a coding app:
  Claude, Claude Cowork and Claude Code; ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex.
- The model is one part. Around it sit context and memory, tools, permissions,
  and checks. Those parts make it useful, reliable and safe.
- An assistant can run with no computer (it works with what you paste), on your
  computer (it works with your files while you watch), or on its own computer
  (it keeps going on a schedule). In class, we worked on our own computers.

## The dashboard build

The practice used a fictional HVAC company with six branches and two weekly
reports. The follow-along page is links.obney.ai/c2-build. The loop was:
describe what you want, look at the result, ask for changes.

1. Start with the problem. Save one report and ask to see it.
2. Test a hunch. Add the staffing report and put both on one timeline.
3. Check what the dashboard claims. Trace a number back to its rows, and check
   that combining the files did not drop, repeat or zero-fill any rows.
4. Ask where next Monday's data comes from. A report arrives, something starts
   the work, the files get checked, the dashboard refreshes, and a person
   reviews it. Each step needs a connection, permission and a place to run.

## What was underneath

- The assistant wrote code to build the dashboard. AI can write a tool that
  runs by fixed rules, or a tool that asks a model each time. How a tool was
  built is different from how it runs.
- **The ladder** has five rungs: Thought Partner & Assistant, AI Workflows,
  Tool Builder, Bots & Agents, and Systems of Record. Rungs 1 and 2 make the
  assistant yours. Rungs 3 to 5 are for other people to rely on. Past that line,
  someone has to own the tool and fix it.
- Once people rely on a tool, three things change. It needs a host: an
  always-on computer you rent. It needs sign-in, because a link someone can
  reach is different from permission to use it. And the stakes go up, so they
  decide who should own the work.

## The case studies

One dental-education company built most of these. Its two builders were the
COO and a CPA, and neither is an engineer.

- **Connectors:** one sentence reaches twelve business systems. The question
  to settle is whose permissions the model uses.
- **Tool Builder:** CPR instructor onboarding, Job Shadow verification and
  Certificate Cleanup. A staff member or student starts each one. The handouts
  tell each story on one page.
- **Bots & Agents:** Ava is a bot that texts new leads and books calls with no
  one pressing a button. Carl is obney.ai's own AI chief of staff. He works from
  a large set of connected notes, so context carries from task to task.

## Before we closed

- Things people rely on grow faster than the number of people who can fix them.
- Three roles share this work: an operator who runs the business and builds the
  first version, an engineer who makes it safe to run, and a software or AI
  engineer who owns the platform underneath.
- A "paved road" gives builders hosting, sign-in and permissions, data
  connections, monitoring and testing that are already decided.
- At work, learn the tools your company approves and go deep on what is
  allowed. Outside work, build freely.
- Two questions decide who should own a tool: can we run it responsibly, and
  who must check it before the business depends on it?
