# Prompts for Your Company

Session 1 · Foundations of Artificial Intelligence · Dr. Andrew Schwarz

Faculty handout from Session 1, November 13, 2026. The slide numbers match
`andrew-schwarz-slides.pdf` in this folder. The printable copy is

## Slide 5 · Start with your company

Use this introduction once. Continue in the same chat and replace bracketed
text with your example. Your company name is optional.

> I lead a company. Ask me, one question at a time, what we sell and to whom,
> why customers choose us and what supports that view, our main improvement
> priority, and what makes it difficult. Summarize my answers for correction.
> Use plain language and the facts I provide. Ask about missing information,
> label assumptions, and leave unknowns open.

## Slide 35 · How could customer choices change?

> Ask what alternatives our customers consider and what they value. Describe
> two plausible ways AI could change their choices, the implications for us,
> and your assumptions. End with one question for my leadership team.

## Slide 42 · Where could we improve customer experience?

> Ask about one recurring customer frustration and how we handle it. Suggest
> two improvements, including a simpler process change. Explain what customers
> would experience, what AI could contribute, and what people would do. Help me
> choose one to investigate.

## Slide 54 · What would need to change internally?

> For [opportunity], ask how the work happens and which people, information,
> and systems are involved. Map potential changes to strategy, processes, data,
> skills, applications, and governance. Identify the first dependency to
> investigate and who could help.

## Slide 67 · Where is our knowledge getting stuck?

> Ask about a question employees repeatedly ask or information they struggle to
> find, then ask where it lives. Explain how AI might help, what information
> needs attention, and who should maintain it. Suggest one small step I could
> authorize.

## Slide 83 · How could this improve employees' work?

> Ask who our opportunity affects and which tasks frustrate them. Describe how
> work might change, one skill needed, and a concern to investigate. Give me
> three questions to ask employees before proceeding.

## Slide 88 · Examine an AI opportunity

> For [business problem and desired outcome], ask about our workflow and
> constraints. Suggest up to three improvements using appropriate AI or
> conventional automation. Compare benefits, required data, workflow changes,
> human responsibilities, and review. Finish with three unanswered questions.

## Slide 99 · Compare a vague and specific request

First try:

> How can AI improve our customer service?

Then try:

> For [workflow and problem], propose one small AI-assisted experiment to
> achieve [outcome]. Respect [constraints]. Specify data, human approval,
> failure points, and success measures. Label assumptions and avoid invented
> savings.

## Slide 101 · Which direction fits our business?

> Compare automating work, improving employee decisions, personalizing service,
> building AI into offerings, and redesigning human–AI work. Identify two
> directions relevant to our priority. Ask one question to distinguish them,
> then suggest a starting point and what we must learn.

## Slide 116 · What remains a leadership decision?

> Ask what could happen if AI makes a mistake in our opportunity. Distinguish
> what AI may prepare, what employees review, and what leaders approve. Suggest
> accountability and escalation arrangements. Identify assumptions behind the
> proposed boundaries.

## Slide 120 · What could we test in 30 days?

> Ask what resources we can commit and how we judge current performance. Draft
> a small experiment with scope, employees, data, human review, and success
> measures. Identify reasons to continue, redesign, or stop, and the CEO
> decision needed.

## Slide 121 · Challenge the proposed initiative

> Review [our opportunity and experiment] as a sponsor, operator, skeptic, and
> steward. Ask one difficult question about value, workflow, assumptions, and
> governance, respectively. Identify the evidence needed and suggest one
> revision. Our group will make the final decision.

## Evaluate each response

Identify one useful insight, one assumption, and one fact to verify.
