# Examples to explore

Five fictional practice scenarios inspired by broad work themes from the first
Academy cohort. These illustrate possibilities, not individual participants,
verified results or ready-to-deploy solutions. All sample figures below are invented.

## Quick menu

- **A. Monthly operating review:** turn numbers and team updates into a useful management briefing.
- **B. Proposal drafting:** prepare a draft from job details, rates and approved terms.
- **C. Project profitability:** bring reports together to understand costs, payments and what needs attention.
- **D. Recurring invoicing:** prepare draft invoices from activity records and billing rules.
- **E. Knowledge workspace:** find and build on meeting notes, decisions and reference material.

Choose something you're curious about. Say “Tell me more about C” or use a title.

## A. Monthly operating review

A department manager gathers figures and updates for a leadership briefing.
AI could organize the material, compare periods and draft questions to investigate.
The manager checks the figures and explanations before sharing the review.

**A small illustration:** This month's completed jobs are 80; last month's were
100. A team update says two staff were away. A draft might say: “Completed jobs
fell by 20 (20%). Staffing may be relevant, but we need more information to
explain the change.” It should not state that absences caused the decline.

**Context lesson:** A comparison period helps show what changed. Team notes add
possible explanations, while evidence is still needed to establish a cause.

An optional invitation: “Want to see how this changes if we also give it the
questions leadership needs the briefing to answer?”

## B. Proposal drafting

A team prepares proposals from job details, rates and approved terms. AI could
assemble a first draft and flag missing information for a person to resolve.
Prices, calculations and commitments need checking before a proposal is sent.

**A small illustration:** The fictional request is for two workshops. The supplied
rate is $600 per workshop; travel is not specified. A draft can show a workshop
subtotal of $1,200 and mark travel as “to confirm.” It cannot assume travel is
included or invent a price for it.

**Context lesson:** Current rates and approved wording let the assistant draft
specific content. A reviewed example gives it a useful structure. Missing terms
should remain visible.

An optional invitation: “Want to see a short draft with the missing detail flagged?”

## C. Project profitability

A project manager brings information from different reports together to see
how jobs are doing and which questions deserve attention. AI could help organize
those inputs and draft a view; matching and calculations need checking.

**A small illustration:** One report lists Project 104's agreed fee as $50,000.
Another lists invoices issued as $30,000. A third shows consultant payments of
$18,000. An assistant could align those rows by project ID and flag what is
missing. Subtracting those payments from invoices does not establish profit:
we have not supplied all costs, revenue definitions or matching reporting periods.

**Context lesson:** More reports can be useful when we know which rows and periods
belong together and what each figure means. A useful result can be a clear
question about missing information, as well as an answer.

An optional invitation: “Want to see the simple combined view and the questions
it would flag for the manager?”

## D. Recurring invoicing

An administrator prepares invoices from activity records and billing rules.
An experiment could produce drafts for review, flagging incomplete records.
Nothing is sent automatically.

**A small illustration:** The supplied rule is $75 per completed service visit.
A fictional record lists four completed visits and one canceled visit. A draft
can show four visits totaling $300. If no cancellation rule is supplied, it
should flag the canceled visit for review rather than invent a charge.

**Context lesson:** Explicit rules and complete activity records make the draft
checkable. A person still verifies the records, rule and result.

An optional invitation: “Want to see how a missing visit date would appear in
the review list?”

## E. Knowledge workspace

A leader uses meeting notes, decisions and reference material to answer questions
and continue work. The Academy folder gives participants a small version of this
experience: an assistant can read relevant files and point to its sources.

**A small illustration:** A fictional Monday note says a launch is planned for
June 10. A Thursday decision note explicitly changes it to June 17. Asked for
the agreed date, the assistant should use June 17 and identify Thursday's
supporting passage. Without the second note, it could only report the older plan.

**Context lesson:** Source access, dates and explicit decisions affect the answer.
Saved notes can be read in a fresh conversation when the folder is accessible.

An optional invitation: “Want to try a question against the actual Academy
schedule and see which passage supports the answer?”

## Guidance for the companion

Keep the A–E labels stable and read the selected example. Give a small illustration
at an introductory level before suggesting implementation. Use one context
lesson and, if helpful, one invitation to explore further. Do not dump every
possible input, tool or risk into the first exchange.

Session 1 is a taste of what working with AI could look like. Their role and
learning hopes can guide the explanation, but do not assume a chosen example
is their workflow. Do not default to “Do you have this process?” or ask for
uploads. Follow curiosity and honor requests to switch examples.

If they explicitly bring their own situation, keep their facts separate from
invented details. When saving an example exploration, label it fictional and
record actual learning or open questions. Do not invent participant reactions
or make a project commitment on their behalf. After Session 2, an explicit
facilitator or participant transition opens the conversation about their own
needs for Sessions 3–5. See AGENTS.md for the learning sequence.
