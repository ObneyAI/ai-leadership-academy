# Examples to explore

Five fictional practice scenarios inspired by broad work themes from the first
Academy cohort. These are possibilities to explore, not accounts of particular
participants, verified results or ready-to-deploy solutions.

## Quick menu

- **A. Monthly operating review:** turn numbers and team updates into a useful management briefing.
- **B. Proposal drafting:** prepare a draft from job details, rates and approved terms.
- **C. Project profitability:** bring reports together to understand costs, payments and what needs attention.
- **D. Recurring invoicing:** prepare draft invoices from activity records and billing rules.
- **E. Knowledge workspace:** find and build on meeting notes, decisions and reference material.

Say “Tell me more about C,” choose another letter, or bring your own idea.

## A. Monthly operating review

A department manager collects monthly figures and updates, then prepares a
review for leadership. AI could help organize the material into a consistent
draft, compare periods and suggest questions for the manager to investigate.
The manager checks the figures and explanations before sharing the review.

Useful context includes the source reports, comparison period, metric definitions,
an example of a useful review and the decisions the audience needs to make.

Connect it to their work: “Do you prepare a recurring update that helps someone
decide what needs attention in your department or business?”

## B. Proposal drafting

A team repeatedly assembles proposals from job details, customer-specific rates
and approved terms. A possible first step is a draft proposal assembled from
those inputs, with missing information flagged for a person to resolve.
Calculations and terms still need appropriate checks before it is sent.

Useful context includes a reviewed example, current rates, scope, approved wording
and what may vary between jobs. Never invent a price or a contractual commitment.

Connect it to their work: “Do you repeatedly prepare a document where much of
the structure stays the same, but the details change for each customer or job?”

## C. Project profitability

A project manager pulls contract amounts, fees, invoices and consultant payments
from different reports to understand how jobs are doing. A possible dashboard
could bring the information together and highlight changes or items to investigate.
AI could help design that view or explain a verified comparison; calculations
and data matching still need checking against the sources.

Useful context includes which reports belong together, the reporting period,
project identifiers, definitions of the figures and what decisions the view supports.
The same pattern can apply to a department or business without project accounting.

Connect it to their work: “Do you have a recurring process where you pull reports
from different places and put them together to make decisions for your department
or business?”

If yes, follow their answer toward one real instance, such as the report they
prepare and what they need it to reveal. Do not ask for every input and decision
at once. If no, offer to explore the fictional example or another option.

## D. Recurring invoicing

An administrator prepares monthly invoices using activity records and billing
rules. A first experiment could organize those inputs, calculate draft amounts
using explicit rules and prepare documents for review. Missing or conflicting
information would be flagged rather than filled in by guesswork.

Useful context includes a sample activity record, current billing rules, a reviewed
invoice and the checks needed before sending. Nothing is sent automatically.

Connect it to their work: “Is there a monthly administrative task where you use
the same rules to prepare a batch of documents or records?”

## E. Knowledge workspace

A leader has meeting notes, past decisions and reference material scattered
across files. An assistant with access to selected sources could help answer
questions, locate the supporting passage and carry useful context into new work.
The Academy folder is a small place to experience that pattern directly.

Useful context includes the actual notes, their dates, terminology and which
sources are current. Missing sources limit what can be answered reliably.

Connect it to their work: “Do you often need to find something your team already
discussed or decided, but spend time looking for the right note or document?”

## Guidance for the companion

Keep the A–E labels stable. Interpret a letter or title using this file. Read the
selected example before discussing it. Explain the work and a possible AI role
briefly, then ask one natural connection question, adapted to what the participant
has already told you. The prompts above are examples, not a mandatory interview.

An example selection is curiosity, not a commitment to a project. If the participant
shares a similar process, develop that real process using their details. If they
only want to understand the example, stay with it and offer a small fictional
illustration. Do not force uploads, a personal use case or a save step.

Explain how useful context would change the help, without promising automation,
access or accuracy before checking. Do not turn suggestions into historical
success claims or invent participant identities, metrics or testimonials.

Keep fictional details separate from the participant's own facts. If they choose
to save a worked example, label it fictional. If their own starting point emerges,
follow the ordinary review/save process and preserve what they actually said.
